Module 1: How Amazon Works for Sellers

The Amazon Marketplace and Where Sellers Fit 14 min

Before you spend a dollar, you need a clear picture of how Amazon is built. Amazon is two businesses in one storefront. In the first, Amazon buys products wholesale from brands and sells them itself. This is called first party, and brands manage it through a separate system called Vendor Central by invitation. In the second, independent businesses list their own products and Amazon provides the customers, the website, the payments, and optionally the warehouses and delivery. This is called third party, and it is what this course is about. You manage it in a tool called Seller Central.

A majority of the units sold on Amazon now come from independent sellers like the people taking this course. That matters for you, because it means Amazon is actively built to support sellers, and it also means you are competing with thousands of them.

The catalog and the ASIN

Amazon keeps one catalog. Every product gets one product page, identified by a number called an ASIN. If you want to sell a product that already exists, you do not create a new page. You attach your offer to the existing page. That means many sellers can be selling the exact same item on the exact same page at the same time.

Why that matters: the Featured Offer

When several sellers offer the same product, Amazon shows one offer in the main purchase box on the page, the Add to Cart and Buy Now buttons. This was long called the Buy Box, and Amazon now also calls it the Featured Offer. Over 80 percent of purchases happen through it. Everything else on the page, including other sellers listed under See All Buying Options, gets a small share of sales. Winning that position is one of the central skills in this course, and Module 9 is dedicated to it.

The three things Amazon gives you

  • Traffic: shoppers already searching to buy, which is the hardest thing for any new business to create.
  • Trust: customers buy from Amazon, so they trust the checkout, the returns, and the delivery promise.
  • Infrastructure: payments, customer service tools, and, if you choose, warehousing and shipping.

What Amazon expects in return

Amazon expects you to protect the customer experience. You ship on time, you describe products accurately, you handle problems quickly, and you follow its policies. Sellers who do this are rewarded with visibility and the Featured Offer. Sellers who do not are limited or suspended. Think of Amazon as a partner with strict standards, not as a place where you can do whatever you want.

Selling Models and Fulfillment Options Explained 15 min

There are two separate decisions every Amazon seller makes, and beginners often mix them up. The first is where your products come from, which is called your sourcing model. The second is who packs and ships the order to the customer, which is your fulfillment method. This lesson covers fulfillment. Module 4 covers sourcing.

Fulfillment by Amazon, or FBA

You send your inventory to Amazon warehouses, which are called fulfillment centers. When a customer orders, Amazon picks, packs, and ships it, handles customer service and returns for that order, and your offer carries the Prime badge. You pay fees for fulfillment and storage. FBA offers shoppers the fast delivery they expect and is commonly reported to win the Featured Offer three to five times more often than merchant fulfilled offers on the same listing.

Fulfillment by Merchant, or FBM

You keep the inventory, or arrange for a supplier to hold it, and you ship every order yourself. You pay no Amazon storage or fulfillment fees, but you carry the responsibility for speed, packaging, tracking, and returns. Late shipments and missing tracking hurt your account health directly.

Seller Fulfilled Prime

If your own shipping is consistently fast, Amazon can qualify you to show the Prime badge on offers you ship yourself. It is demanding, but it lets strong operators keep control of their inventory and still get the Prime advantage.

Multi Channel Fulfillment and Warehousing and Distribution

Multi Channel Fulfillment lets Amazon ship orders that come from other places, such as your own website or other marketplaces, out of your FBA inventory. Amazon Warehousing and Distribution is a lower cost storage service for bulk inventory that replenishes your FBA stock as needed. Both are growth tools, not starting tools.

Where dropshipping fits

Dropshipping is a sourcing and shipping arrangement where a supplier ships the order directly to your customer. On Amazon it is a form of merchant fulfillment, so it is judged on the same speed and tracking standards, and it has special rules covered in Module 6.

Choosing for now

A simple rule for beginners: use FBM or dropshipping to test products with little cash at risk, and use FBA for products that prove they sell and that you want to scale. Many successful sellers do both at once, depending on the product.

Module 2: Business Foundations

Legal and Financial Setup Before You Sell 13 min

This lesson is general education, not legal or tax advice. Rules differ by state and by situation, so confirm your choices with a licensed accountant or attorney. The goal here is to help you ask the right questions.

Choose how your business is structured

You can start as a sole proprietor, which is simple and uses your Social Security number, or form a limited liability company, an LLC, which separates your business from your personal assets and is the choice many sellers make once real money is involved. Amazon accepts both. If you form an LLC, register it in your state and keep it in good standing.

Get an EIN

An Employer Identification Number is a free tax ID from the IRS. Even sole proprietors can get one, and using it instead of your Social Security number in business paperwork is a sensible privacy step. Apply only through the official IRS site, because paid third party sites charge for something that is free.

Open a separate business bank account

Keep every business dollar in one account. This makes taxes easier, protects your LLC status, and gives Amazon one clean place to deposit your earnings. Amazon will ask for bank details during registration.

Resale certificate and sales tax basics

If you buy inventory from wholesalers, they may ask for a resale certificate so you do not pay sales tax on goods you will resell. Amazon collects and remits sales tax for you in most states under marketplace facilitator laws, but you may still have your own obligations in some situations. Module 12 covers this in more detail.

Insurance

Amazon requires many sellers to carry commercial general liability insurance once their sales reach certain monthly levels, and it can require proof. Even before that requirement, product liability coverage protects you if a product you sold causes harm. Ask an insurance agent who works with online sellers, and check Seller Central for the current requirement.

Keep records from day one

Save every supplier invoice, receipt, and bank statement. You will need them for taxes, for Amazon category approvals, and for proving where your inventory came from if Amazon asks.

Startup Budget and Realistic Expectations 12 min

Most people who quit Amazon quit because they ran out of money before they learned the system. A realistic budget protects you from that.

Typical startup ranges

  • Dropshipping or merchant fulfilled testing: roughly $500 to $1,500 to start.
  • Wholesale buying from brands or distributors: often $5,000 to $25,000 or more, because you buy inventory up front.
  • Private label, where you create your own branded product: often $3,000 to $10,000 or more, covering samples, production, branding, shipping, and launch advertising.

These are common ranges, not promises. Your number depends on the category and product cost.

Costs people forget

  • The Professional selling plan, $39.99 per month.
  • Supplier or dropshipping platform subscriptions.
  • Amazon fees on every sale, which can be 8 to 15 percent in most categories for referral fees alone, plus fulfillment and storage if you use FBA.
  • Advertising, because new listings usually need paid traffic to get started.
  • Returns, damaged units, and unsold inventory.
  • Taxes, which are due on profit, not on cash in your account.

Cash flow and timing

Amazon pays out on a regular schedule, and funds from new sellers can be held for a period. Plan for money to be tied up in inventory and in transit. A common beginner mistake is spending the entire budget on the first order of inventory.

Set honest expectations

Amazon selling is a real business, not a shortcut. Many sellers see slow first months while they learn, gather reviews, and tune their ads. This course gives you the knowledge, but results depend on your product choices, your execution, your budget, and the market. No one can guarantee income, and you should be wary of anyone who does. Set a testing budget you can afford to lose, treat early sales as learning, and measure everything.

Module 3: Setting Up Your Seller Account

Choosing Your Selling Plan and Preparing Your Documents 11 min

Amazon offers two plans. Choosing correctly saves money and avoids a limitation you would have to fix later.

Individual plan

No monthly fee, but Amazon charges $0.99 for every item you sell, on top of the usual selling fees. It is designed for people selling very small quantities. It has limited tools and does not give you access to advertising, and Individual sellers are not eligible to win the Featured Offer.

Professional plan

$39.99 per month with no per item fee. It unlocks bulk listing tools, reports, advertising, Brand Registry eligibility, and the ability to win the Featured Offer. If you plan to sell more than about 40 items a month, or you want to grow at all, Professional is the better value. You can switch from Individual to Professional later.

What to prepare before you start

Gather these first so registration takes minutes instead of days.

  • A government issued photo ID, such as a passport or driver license, that matches your legal name.
  • Your business bank account details, where Amazon will deposit your earnings.
  • A credit card that can be charged internationally, for your subscription and any account charges.
  • Your Social Security number or EIN, and your tax information.
  • A phone number you can answer for two step verification, and an email address you control.
  • Proof of address, such as a recent utility bill or bank statement, in case Amazon requests it.

Keep details consistent

The name on your ID, your bank account, your card, and your tax record should match what you enter. Inconsistencies are the number one reason verification stalls. Use your own legal information only, and never open an account using someone else's identity.

Step by Step Registration and Your First Look at Seller Central 15 min

Registration usually takes 15 to 30 minutes of typing. Verification afterward can take 24 hours to several days, and Amazon may ask for extra documents. Follow these steps in order.

Registration steps

  • Go to the official Amazon seller site and choose to sign up. Create an account with a business email you control.
  • Enter your business type and legal business information exactly as it appears on your documents.
  • Add your primary contact and your phone number, and complete the two step verification.
  • Enter the bank account where earnings will be deposited and the credit card for fees.
  • Enter your tax information.
  • Complete identity verification by uploading your ID, and often by joining a short live video call with an Amazon verifier. Have your ID in hand and sit in a quiet, well lit room.
  • Choose your store name. It can be your business name, and it appears on your seller profile.
  • Review and submit, then wait for Amazon to confirm your account is open.

If verification is slow or fails

Check your email, including spam, for requests from Amazon. Reply with clear, readable documents that match your registration details. Do not create a second account to get around a problem, because duplicate accounts can be banned.

Your first tour of Seller Central

Once approved, spend an hour exploring before you list anything.

  • Inventory: where you create listings and track stock.
  • Orders: where you manage and confirm shipments.
  • Advertising: where campaigns are built.
  • Reports and Payments: where you track sales, fees, and deposits.
  • Performance and Account Health: where Amazon scores you. Learn this early, because it decides whether your account survives.
  • Settings: where you set shipping options, returns, and notifications.

Turn on the safety basics

Enable two step verification, set up a dedicated email for Amazon, set your return address, and review your notification settings so you never miss a policy message.

Module 4: Sourcing Models

Dropshipping, Wholesale, Arbitrage, and Private Label Compared 16 min

Your sourcing model is how you get products to sell. Each model has different cost, risk, speed, and profit potential. Understanding all four helps you pick a starting point and plan where to go next.

Dropshipping

A supplier holds the inventory and ships orders directly to your customers. You carry almost no inventory cost, so startup can be roughly $500 to $1,500 and you can launch within weeks. The tradeoffs are thin margins, often 15 to 30 percent before advertising, little control over shipping speed and quality, and strict Amazon rules, which Module 6 covers in full.

Wholesale

You buy goods in bulk from brands or authorized distributors and resell them. Because you buy genuine, branded product, quality is predictable. Upfront cost is higher. Many brands also restrict who can sell them, so you may need approvals, and you often compete with other sellers on the same listing for the Featured Offer.

Online and retail arbitrage

You buy discounted products from stores and resell them on Amazon at a higher price. It can work with a small budget, but it needs constant sourcing work, risks brand and category restrictions, and depends on promotions that disappear.

Private label

You have a manufacturer make a product that you brand as your own. You own the listing, so you avoid price wars with other sellers on the same page, and margins can reach 30 to 50 percent or more. It takes the longest, about 90 days to launch, costs the most, roughly $3,000 to $10,000 or more, and means you carry inventory and product risk.

A practical path

Many new sellers test demand with merchant fulfilled or dropshipped products, learn how listings, ads, and customer service work with little risk, and then move proven products into wholesale or private label. Choose the model that matches your budget and your appetite for risk, and be willing to change models as you learn.

Finding and Vetting Legitimate Suppliers 13 min

Your supplier is a business partner whose mistakes become your problems. A late or damaged shipment hurts your metrics, not theirs. Vet suppliers carefully.

Where legitimate suppliers come from

  • Manufacturers and brands that sell wholesale through their own websites.
  • Authorized distributors that carry multiple brands.
  • Established dropshipping platforms that screen suppliers, covered in Module 6.
  • Trade shows and industry directories for larger buyers.

Vetting checklist

  • Ask for a business license or registration and verify the company exists.
  • Confirm they are the manufacturer or an authorized distributor, not another reseller.
  • Order samples yourself and inspect quality, packaging, and delivery time.
  • Ask about return and damage policies, minimum orders, and who pays for defects.
  • Confirm they can ship with your business name and without their own branding when needed.
  • Check stock reliability, because running out of stock after you take an order is a cancellation against you.
  • Start with a small test order before committing.

Keep proof of purchase

Amazon can ask for invoices showing you sourced genuine, authorized product. Always receive and save an invoice with the supplier name, address, product, quantity, and date. Invoices must be from a real supplier and must not be altered.

Red flags

Be cautious of suppliers who refuse invoices, price far below market with no explanation, ask for payment by gift card or unusual methods, or promise guaranteed profits. If a deal sounds too good to be true on genuine branded goods, it is often counterfeit or gray market inventory, and selling counterfeit goods can end your account.

Module 5: Product Research and Profit Math

How to Research Products Worth Selling 15 min

Choosing the product is the biggest decision you will make. A great listing cannot rescue a product nobody wants, and a mediocre listing can still sell a product people badly need. Research first, then buy.

What you are looking for

  • Steady demand: shoppers search for it all year, or in a predictable seasonal pattern.
  • Manageable competition: not dominated by a handful of huge brands with thousands of reviews.
  • Room for profit after every fee, with a healthy gap between your cost and the selling price.
  • Small, light, durable items, which cost less to ship and break less often.
  • No gating or heavy restrictions, so you can actually list it.

Use Amazon itself first

Search the products you are considering. Look at the Best Sellers lists in the category, the autocomplete suggestions as you type, and the first page of results. Note prices, review counts, and how new the top listings are. If new listings with few reviews are on page one, the category is open to newcomers.

Read the sales rank

Every product shows a Best Sellers Rank. A lower number means more sales in that category. Compare ranks across competing products to estimate demand. Many sellers also use paid research tools that estimate monthly sales, search volume, and trends. These are helpful but they are estimates, so cross check them against what you see on Amazon.

Study the reviews

Read the three star and one star reviews of top products. Complaints tell you what customers wish was better, which is where you can compete or where you should avoid a product entirely if the problem cannot be fixed.

Avoid these traps

  • Branded or licensed goods you have no authorization to sell.
  • Products that need certifications, such as electronics, children's items, or anything ingestible, until you understand the rules in Module 11.
  • Fragile, oversized, or heavy items with high shipping cost.
  • Fads that will be gone in a month.
  • Products with many suppliers and a race to the lowest price.

Profit Math: Know Your Numbers Before You Buy 16 min

A product that looks profitable can lose money once every cost is counted. The only way to know is to do the math before you buy a single unit.

The costs to include

  • Product cost, including any minimum order effect.
  • Shipping from the supplier to you or to Amazon.
  • Amazon referral fee, which is a percentage of the price. It is 15 percent in most categories and ranges from 8 percent to 45 percent depending on the category, usually with a small minimum per item.
  • Fulfillment cost: either FBA fees, or your own packing and shipping if you fulfill yourself.
  • Storage and inbound fees if you use FBA, and the low inventory fee if you run too lean.
  • Advertising cost.
  • Returns, damages, and a small buffer for unexpected costs.

Worked example one: merchant fulfilled or dropshipped

This example uses round numbers for illustration, not actual current prices.

  • Selling price: $30.00
  • Supplier cost, including their shipping to the customer: $15.00
  • Referral fee at 15 percent: $4.50
  • Advertising allowance at 10 percent: $3.00
  • Profit before taxes: $30.00 minus $22.50 equals $7.50, a margin of 25 percent.

Worked example two: FBA

Again an illustration. Look up the real fees for your exact product with Amazon's revenue calculator.

  • Selling price: $30.00
  • Product landed cost at your warehouse or prep center: $8.00
  • Referral fee at 15 percent: $4.50
  • FBA fulfillment fee for a small standard item, example: $4.00
  • Storage, inbound, and prep allowance: $1.00
  • Advertising allowance at 10 percent: $3.00
  • Profit before taxes: $30.00 minus $20.50 equals $9.50, a margin of about 32 percent.

Break even and advertising

Your break even advertising cost of sale, ACoS, equals your profit margin before ads. If your margin before ads is 35 percent, any ad campaign with an ACoS above 35 percent loses money on each sale. Knowing this number prevents the most common way new sellers burn through cash.

Check the numbers with Amazon's own calculator

Amazon provides a free revenue calculator that estimates referral and fulfillment fees for an exact product. Use it on every product before you decide. Rule of thumb for beginners: aim for at least 25 to 30 percent margin before ads, so there is room to advertise and absorb surprises.

Validating and Choosing Your First Product 12 min

Now combine the research and the math into one decision. Do not fall in love with a product. Make it earn its place.

A simple scorecard

Rate each candidate from one to five on these points and pick the best total.

  • Demand: steady searches and healthy sales rank.
  • Competition: room for a newcomer.
  • Margin: at least 25 to 30 percent before ads.
  • Simplicity: small, light, durable, easy to describe.
  • Access: no gating, no brand restriction, no certification hurdle.
  • Supplier reliability: stock, speed, and quality you have checked.

Validate before you commit

Order a sample and check quality. Search for a supplier alternative so you are not stuck with one source. Test the price against competitors by calculating what you would earn at a price that wins the Featured Offer, not your wishful price.

Start small

For your first product, buy or list the smallest quantity that lets you learn. Your goal in the first 30 to 60 days is data: how people find the listing, how often they buy, how you handle orders, and what the true profit is.

Decide what success looks like

Write down a target before you launch, such as a minimum number of sales and a minimum margin after fees. If the product meets it, scale. If it does not, change the price, listing, or ads. If it still fails, move on without regret. Testing products quickly and cheaply is the real skill.

Module 6: Dropshipping the Compliant Way

Amazon Dropshipping Policy in Plain Language 14 min

Amazon allows dropshipping, but only under strict conditions. Breaking them is one of the fastest ways to lose a seller account. This lesson explains the rules in plain language. Always read the current policy in Seller Central, because it is the final authority.

The core rules

  • You must be the seller of record. Your business must appear as the seller to the customer.
  • You must remove any supplier, retailer, or third party names, logos, and prices from packing slips, invoices, and outer packaging. The package must show you as the seller.
  • You are responsible for returns and customer service, even when a supplier ships the order.
  • You must comply with the rest of Amazon's seller policies and terms.

What is not allowed

You may not buy a product from another retailer, such as a large department store or an online marketplace, and have that retailer ship it straight to your Amazon customer. The retailer's packing slip and branding would identify the wrong seller, and you would not control the customer experience. Sellers who do this commonly face suspension, and the penalty for repeated violations can be permanent.

What is allowed

Working with a manufacturer, wholesaler, or distributor that ships to your customers in blank or custom packaging that carries your business as the seller. That is why choosing the right supplier is the heart of compliant dropshipping.

Why Amazon cares

Amazon wants the customer to know exactly who sold the item and who is accountable if something goes wrong. A package from an unfamiliar company with a different name creates confusion, and confusion creates complaints.

The speed problem

Even a compliant supplier can sink you if shipping is slow. Amazon expects late shipments under 4 percent and cancellations before shipping under 2.5 percent. Suppliers that ship from overseas in 7 to 20 days make it hard to meet delivery promises, which is why many sellers prefer suppliers with warehouses in the United States.

Protect yourself

Keep written agreements with suppliers that confirm blank packaging and your seller name, keep invoices, test order the full process yourself, and watch your account health daily.

Dropshipping Supplier Platforms and How to Choose 15 min

Dropshipping platforms connect sellers with suppliers and often automate inventory updates and order forwarding. None of them is the single best choice. The right one depends on your budget, your products, and how fast the items must reach the customer. This lesson describes three widely used options. Details and prices change, so confirm everything on each company's own site before you commit. Mitype is not affiliated with these companies and does not guarantee their service.

Spocket

Spocket curates suppliers, many based in the United States and Europe, and focuses on faster delivery, commonly two to five days. Plans are paid, with entry level pricing around $39.99 per month at the time of research. It suits sellers who care about delivery speed and branded invoicing. Fewer products are available than on larger catalog platforms.

CJDropshipping

CJDropshipping offers a very large catalog of 400,000 or more products, a free subscription, and warehouses in the United States and other regions. Many products ship from China, with delivery often 7 to 20 days, and faster when stock is held in a local warehouse. It also offers custom packaging and product sourcing. It is popular for testing and for private label, but you must check shipping time per product against Amazon's expectations.

Doba

Doba connects sellers to more than 160 supplier integrations, mainly in the United States and China, and automates inventory and price updates across channels including Amazon. It uses a paid subscription and suits sellers building a larger catalog who want one dashboard.

How to choose

  • Delivery time from the supplier location to your customers.
  • Whether packaging and invoices can carry your business name.
  • Product quality, which you confirm by ordering samples.
  • Stock accuracy and how quickly the platform syncs inventory.
  • Total cost, including subscription, product price, and shipping.
  • Return handling and support responsiveness.

Test before you trust

Place a real order with each candidate, shipped to yourself, and inspect the box, the packing slip, the speed, and the tracking. If the box shows another company's name, it is not compliant for Amazon.

Your Dropshipping Order Workflow From Sale to Delivery 14 min

A written workflow keeps you compliant when you are busy. Here is a reliable routine you can adapt.

Before the sale

  • List only products your supplier has in stock, and keep your inventory quantities updated or automated.
  • Set your handling time honestly, based on how long your supplier needs to ship.
  • Price with your full costs included, including supplier shipping.

When an order arrives

  • Check the order in Seller Central and confirm the shipping address.
  • Place the order with your supplier right away, using the customer's address, and confirm that the packing slip and packaging will show your business as the seller.
  • Record the supplier order number.

Tracking and confirmation

  • As soon as the supplier ships, get the tracking number and confirm shipment in Seller Central with the correct carrier. Every shipment needs verifiable tracking regardless of carrier.
  • Watch for delays. If tracking stalls, contact the supplier at once and contact the customer before they contact you.

Problems and returns

  • Respond to every customer message within 24 hours.
  • Decide in advance who pays for return shipping and how returned goods are handled with each supplier.
  • Never tell a customer to contact the supplier. As seller of record, the customer service is yours.

Cancellations

If your supplier runs out of stock, cancel before shipping only when necessary, because pre fulfillment cancellations count against you. This is why accurate stock levels matter.

Review weekly

Each week check your late shipment rate, cancellation rate, defect rate, and customer messages. Fix small problems before they become account health problems.

Module 7: FBA Step by Step

How FBA Works and What It Costs 14 min

FBA means you send inventory to Amazon and Amazon fulfills your orders. It is the engine behind the fast delivery shoppers expect, and this is the model that matches shipping products to Amazon facilities.

The flow

  • You source inventory from a supplier or manufacturer.
  • You prepare and label each unit to Amazon's standards.
  • You create a shipment in Seller Central and send it to the fulfillment center Amazon assigns.
  • Amazon stores your units and, when customers order, picks, packs, ships, and handles customer service and returns for those orders.
  • Amazon deposits your earnings after subtracting fees.

The fees to expect

  • Referral fee, a percentage of the sale price by category.
  • FBA fulfillment fee, based on size and weight. Amazon raised average fulfillment fees by about $0.08 per unit on January 15, 2026.
  • Monthly storage fees, which are higher in the fourth quarter holiday season.
  • Inbound placement fees, which apply when you send inventory to a single location instead of splitting it. Amazon restructured these in January 2026.
  • A low inventory level fee, which applies when you hold too little stock compared to demand. The threshold reported for 2026 is about 35 days of supply.
  • Aged inventory and removal charges for units that sit too long or that you ask Amazon to return.

Is FBA right for your product?

FBA works best for products that sell steadily, are small and light, and have margins that can absorb the fees. It works poorly for slow sellers, bulky items, or low priced items where fees consume the profit. Always run the profit math before sending inventory.

Know the 2026 changes

Amazon ended its own prep and labeling services in the United States on January 1, 2026, and reported plans to end commingled inventory in 2026. Always confirm current rules in Seller Central before you ship.

Preparing and Sending Inventory to Amazon 16 min

This lesson walks through getting inventory into a fulfillment center. Because Amazon no longer prepares or labels units for you, accuracy matters. Units that arrive wrong can be refused or charged defect fees.

Step one: list the product

Create or match the listing in Seller Central and choose FBA as the fulfillment method.

Step two: use Send to Amazon

Amazon's Send to Amazon workflow guides every inbound shipment. The main steps are choosing the products and quantities, preparing the products, labeling the inventory, reviewing the shipment, choosing a shipping method, and confirming delivery.

Step three: prepare each unit

  • Make sure each unit is sealed and protected. Fragile items need real cushioning.
  • Liquids and items that can leak must be properly sealed.
  • Units sold as a set must be packaged and labeled as one item.
  • Follow category specific prep rules shown in Seller Central.

Step four: label correctly

Every unit must be scannable. You can apply Amazon's FNSKU barcode label to each unit, or in limited cases rely on a manufacturer barcode where Amazon allows it. Labels must scan the first time, sit on a flat spot, and not cover other barcodes. Many sellers hire a prep center to receive supplier goods, label, inspect, and forward them to Amazon for a per unit fee.

Step five: ship

You can use Amazon's partnered carrier program, which usually offers discounted rates, or arrange your own carrier. Small shipments go by parcel, and larger ones go by pallet or freight. Print the shipment labels and box content details, and attach them as instructed.

Step six: track check in

Amazon scans your shipment when it arrives. This can take days or weeks in busy seasons. Watch the shipment status for receiving problems and respond quickly.

Costs of mistakes

Non compliant units can incur defect fees per unit. For standard size items, reported ranges are from about $0.32 to $5.72 per unit, and up to about $8.25 for bulky items. The cure is careful preparation and a pilot shipment of a few units before sending a large batch.

Inventory Planning, Restocking, and Storage Limits 12 min

Running out of stock loses sales and ranking. Holding too much stock ties up cash and costs storage fees. Good planning keeps you between the two.

Know your sales velocity

Track how many units you sell per day over the last 30 days. Divide your stock on hand by that daily number to see how many days of supply you have.

Plan for lead time

Add up the time to order from your supplier, produce, ship to you or a prep center, prepare, ship to Amazon, and get checked in. If that total is 45 days, you must reorder before your stock drops below roughly 45 days plus a safety buffer.

Avoid the low inventory fee and stockouts

Keep enough supply so you do not trigger the low inventory fee, and so you do not lose the Featured Offer when you go out of stock.

Avoid overstock

Excess units pay monthly storage and may incur aged inventory charges. Run promotions, lower the price, or remove units before they become expensive to hold.

Know your storage limits

Amazon sets storage limits based on your performance and sales history. New sellers often have lower limits, so build up gradually and keep your account in good standing.

Seasonal planning

Order early for peak times such as Prime Day events and the holiday season, because Amazon checks in inventory slower when it is busy, and fees can rise. Amazon announced holiday fees would stay flat in 2026, but check current schedules.

Keep a simple tracker

Record each product's stock, daily sales, lead time, and reorder date in one sheet and review it weekly.

Module 8: Listings, Brand Registry, and Reviews

Creating a Listing That Ranks and Converts 16 min

Your listing has two jobs. It must help Amazon match your product to what shoppers search for, and it must persuade a shopper to buy once they land on it.

Product identifiers

Most products need a GTIN, usually a UPC. Barcodes bought from the official standards organization are accepted and protect you from problems later. If a product legitimately has no barcode, such as some handmade or private label goods, Amazon offers a GTIN exemption, which requires the brand to exist in Amazon's system. Never use random or cheap resold barcodes, because they can cause listing removals.

The title

Include the brand, the product type, the key feature, and important details such as size, color, or quantity. Follow Amazon's category style rules, avoid promotional words, and keep it readable.

Bullet points

Lead each bullet with a benefit and then the feature. Answer the questions a buyer would ask, such as size, material, compatibility, and what is included.

Images

Images drive conversion more than almost anything. Use a clean main image on a white background, then add lifestyle images, size comparisons, infographics, and close ups. Use high resolution so zoom works.

Description and backend terms

Write a clear description and fill the backend search terms with relevant words you did not use elsewhere. Do not repeat words unnecessarily and never include competitor brand names.

Price and offer

Set a price that can win the Featured Offer and still protect your margin. Make sure your shipping settings and handling time are honest.

Optimize with data

After launch, watch your sessions and unit session percentage, the share of visitors who buy. If traffic is fine but conversion is weak, improve images, price, or reviews. If traffic is low, improve keywords and advertising.

Brand Registry, A+ Content, and Vine 13 min

If you sell your own brand, Brand Registry gives you tools that make your listings stronger and protect them from copycats.

What you need

You need an active registered trademark for your brand in the country where you enroll, or a pending application that Amazon now accepts in many cases. In the United States this is through the government trademark office. Your brand name must match the trademark exactly, and your brand name or logo must appear on the product or packaging.

What Brand Registry unlocks

  • A+ Content, richer product descriptions with extra images and comparison charts. Reported conversion lifts range from about 3 to 10 percent.
  • Sponsored Brands advertising.
  • Brand protection tools to report counterfeits and listing hijackers.
  • Brand Analytics, with data on what shoppers search for.
  • Manage Your Experiments, for testing images and content.
  • Amazon Vine, where Amazon invites trusted reviewers to review your new product, covered below.

Is it worth it?

For private label sellers, yes. It protects your listing and improves conversion. For sellers of other brands' products, it does not apply, and the brand owner controls it.

Start the trademark early

Trademark applications take time. If you plan a private label product, apply early so you are ready when your product launches.

Watch for lookalikes

Choose a distinctive brand name that is not already taken, and search the trademark database first to avoid rejections or conflicts.

Reviews, Customer Service, and Returns 13 min

Reviews and service decide whether shoppers buy and whether Amazon keeps showing your products.

Rules on reviews

Reviews must come from genuine customers. You may not offer money, free products, discounts, or gifts in exchange for reviews, you may not ask only happy customers to review, and you may not pay for or trade reviews. Violations can lead to account removal. Do not ask family and friends to post reviews on your own products.

Legitimate ways to earn reviews

  • Deliver a good product that arrives as described.
  • Use Amazon's built in Request a Review button, which sends a compliant request after delivery.
  • Use Amazon Vine if your brand is eligible.
  • Include a helpful insert that thanks the buyer and offers support, without asking for a positive review.

Customer messages

Respond within 24 hours, be polite, and be specific. Amazon measures your response time. Do not try to move customers off Amazon, and do not include outside links or promotions.

Returns

Amazon allows customers to return most items. With FBA, Amazon handles returns. With merchant fulfillment, you set a return policy that must meet Amazon's standards, and you handle returned items. Review why items come back, because a pattern points to a product or listing problem you can fix.

Negative feedback and claims

Resolve problems quickly with refunds or replacements when appropriate. Unresolved issues can become A to z claims, which count against your defect rate. You can request removal of feedback that violates Amazon's guidelines, but not simply because it is negative.

Module 9: Winning the Buy Box

How the Buy Box and Featured Offer Work 14 min

The Buy Box is the Add to Cart and Buy Now area on a product page. Amazon now also calls the winning position the Featured Offer. More than 80 percent of purchases happen through it, so on a page shared by many sellers, winning it is the difference between steady sales and almost none.

It rotates

When several qualified sellers offer the same product, Amazon does not pick one winner forever. The Buy Box rotates among eligible offers, giving each a share of the time. Your goal is to qualify and then earn the largest share. Share can change by the hour as prices, stock, and performance change.

Who is eligible

  • You need a Professional selling account.
  • Your account must be in good standing with healthy performance metrics.
  • The product must be in a category and condition that qualifies, usually new.
  • Your offer must be in stock and priced competitively.
  • For new sellers, Amazon may require some sales history before you win much share. Sellers new to a listing often get a limited chance first.

What Amazon weighs

Amazon does not publish an exact formula. The commonly reported factors include fulfillment method, the total landed price including shipping, seller performance metrics, shipping speed, stock level, and whether your listing is healthy and not suppressed. Price is only one factor. A cheaper offer from a seller with poor metrics usually loses to a slightly higher priced offer from a seller with strong metrics.

Fulfillment matters

FBA offers carry a structural advantage because of fast, reliable delivery. FBA sellers are commonly reported to win the Buy Box three to five times more often than merchant fulfilled sellers on the same listing. Merchant fulfilled sellers can still win with fast, tracked, reliable shipping.

Delivery speed is rising in importance

Local delivery speed carries more weight than in the past. A seller who can deliver in one day can beat a cheaper competitor who needs two.

A reality check

You cannot buy the Buy Box or force it. You earn it by combining a competitive price, fast fulfillment, and excellent metrics.

Winning and Keeping the Buy Box 14 min

Use this action list, in this order. Fix the foundation before you adjust price.

1. Protect your metrics

  • Order defect rate under 1 percent.
  • Late shipment rate under 4 percent.
  • Pre fulfillment cancellation rate under 2.5 percent.
  • High valid tracking, meaning every shipment has real, working tracking.

If one of these slips, Amazon can reduce your share across your whole catalog, not just one listing.

2. Use fast, reliable fulfillment

Choose FBA for products where you want the strongest position. If you ship yourself, set honest handling times, ship the same day when possible, and use trackable services.

3. Price to the landed total

Compare your item price plus shipping to the competition. You do not have to be the cheapest, but you should be close to the lowest comparable offers from sellers with similar delivery.

4. Keep stock available

If you go out of stock, you lose your position and may take time to regain it. Reorder in time.

5. Respond fast to buyers

Answer messages within 24 hours.

6. Use repricing carefully

Repricing tools adjust your price automatically based on rules. Set a minimum price that protects your margin, so the tool never pushes you into a loss. Avoid races to the bottom, where two sellers keep undercutting each other until neither makes money.

7. Build your own brand when you can

If you are the only seller of your product, as with private label under Brand Registry, you own the Buy Box on your listing. This is one of the strongest reasons sellers move toward private label.

8. Watch your listing health

A suppressed listing cannot win. Check for suppression alerts in Seller Central and fix them promptly.

Monitor and adjust

Check your Buy Box percentage on the Business Reports page by product. If the percentage drops, look first at stock, metrics, shipping, and price changes by competitors.

Module 10: Amazon Advertising

Setting Up Sponsored Products 15 min

Most new listings need advertising to get seen. Sponsored Products is the most beginner friendly and the most important ad type. These are the ads that appear in search results and on product pages, labeled Sponsored.

How it works

Amazon advertising is a pay per click auction. You pay only when a shopper clicks, not when they see the ad. You bid on keywords, or on specific products, and Amazon shows your ad when it fits.

Key terms

  • ACoS, advertising cost of sale: ad spend divided by ad sales. A 20 percent ACoS means you spent $20 to make $100 in ad sales.
  • ROAS, return on ad spend: ad sales divided by ad spend. It is the same idea turned upside down.
  • Click through rate and conversion rate, which show how well your ad and listing work.
  • Break even ACoS: your profit margin before ads. You lose money above it.

Your first campaign

  • Make sure your listing is complete and competitive before you advertise.
  • Start with one automatic campaign. Amazon chooses the keywords and targets for you, which teaches you what shoppers use.
  • Set a small daily budget you can afford to lose while learning.
  • Use dynamic bidding settings suited to your goal, and start with moderate bids.
  • Let the campaign collect data for one to two weeks before making large changes.

Then move to manual campaigns

Read the search term report from your automatic campaign. Take the search terms that produce sales and place them in a manual campaign with controlled bids. Add terms that cost money without producing sales as negative keywords so you stop paying for them.

Add other ad types later

Sponsored Brands, which need Brand Registry, and Sponsored Display come after your Sponsored Products campaigns are consistently profitable.

The Weekly Optimization Loop and New Creative Tools 12 min

Advertising rewards a steady routine, not constant tinkering. Use this weekly loop.

The weekly loop

  • Review the search term report. Promote winning terms to manual campaigns.
  • Add negative keywords for terms that spent money without sales.
  • Adjust bids. Lower bids on terms with ACoS above your break even, and raise bids on profitable terms that get few impressions.
  • Check budgets so your best campaigns do not run out of budget early in the day.
  • Look at the overall result in your profit, not just ACoS. Sales driven by ads also lift your organic ranking, so a campaign near break even can still help your listing grow.

Launch, stabilize, scale

At launch you spend more to gather data and reviews, accepting low or break even returns. When the listing is stable, you tune for profit. When profit is steady, you scale budgets and add campaigns.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Advertising a listing with weak images, price, or reviews.
  • Using a huge budget before learning what works.
  • Never adding negative keywords.
  • Changing settings every day so no data can build.
  • Ignoring margin and chasing sales volume at a loss.

New creative tools

Amazon announced at its 2026 seller conference that Creative Studio is available to sellers at no charge. It can generate lifestyle images from a product photo, create short video ads from still images, and produce audio ads. Amazon reported an average sales lift of about 12 percent for users, though your results will vary. Try it on a few products and compare results against your existing creative.

Module 11: Compliance, Account Health, and Suspensions

Policies, Restricted Products, and Intellectual Property 15 min

You can lose an account in a day for something you did not know was against the rules. Learn them before you list.

Restricted and gated categories

Some categories require approval before you can sell. Commonly gated areas include certain automotive items, supplements and nutritional products, beauty and personal care, fine jewelry and watches, luxury goods, shoes and handbags, and wine and spirits. Approval often requires supplier invoices, brand authorization letters, or lab testing. Amazon has been tightening documentation, so start collecting invoices early.

Brand gating

Even if a category is open, individual brands can be gated at the request of the brand owner. You may need written authorization from the brand to sell it.

Restricted and hazardous products

Some products cannot be sold at all, and others, such as batteries, aerosols, and flammables, need special handling and safety documents. Check the restricted products list for any item before you buy it.

Safety and certification

Children's products, electronics, and many other items require safety testing and certification. Selling an uncertified product can lead to listing removal and legal exposure.

Intellectual property

  • Never sell counterfeit or unauthorized copies.
  • Do not use another company's trademarked brand name, logos, or images without permission.
  • Do not copy another seller's photos or text.
  • Respect patents and copyrights. A single complaint from a rights owner can suspend a listing, and repeated complaints can suspend an account.

Product condition and authenticity

Sell only genuine, correctly described products. Keep invoices that prove where your inventory came from.

Stay current

Read policy updates in Seller Central, which Amazon sends as notices. Many suspensions happen because a seller never read a policy change.

Account Health, Suspensions, and Plans of Action 15 min

Account health is Amazon's report card for you. Check it every day.

The main performance metrics

  • Order defect rate: under 1 percent.
  • Late shipment rate: under 4 percent.
  • Pre fulfillment cancellation rate: under 2.5 percent.
  • Valid tracking rate: high, because every merchant fulfilled shipment needs verifiable tracking from any carrier.

The Account Health Rating

Amazon also uses a score. As of 2026 guidance, 200 to 1,000 is healthy, 100 to 199 is at risk, and 99 or lower is in the danger zone. Every seller starts at the baseline, and points are deducted when policies are violated or metrics slip, and restored when problems are resolved. Lower scores in the healthy range can draw more automatic review.

Common causes of suspension

  • Poor metrics.
  • Intellectual property or authenticity complaints.
  • Review manipulation.
  • Dropshipping from retailers or hiding the true seller.
  • Selling restricted products.
  • Related accounts, such as opening a second account without permission.

If you are suspended

Stay calm and read the notice carefully. Amazon will tell you the reason and often how to appeal. Do not open a new account.

Writing a plan of action

An appeal usually requires a plan of action with three parts.

  • Root cause: what went wrong, in your own honest words.
  • Corrective actions: what you have already fixed.
  • Preventive measures: what you will do so it does not happen again.

Attach documents such as invoices and proof of changes. Be specific, concise, and respectful. Take responsibility rather than blaming Amazon or buyers. If the first appeal is rejected, read the feedback and revise it.

Prevention is cheaper

Keep your metrics healthy, keep invoices, follow policies, and check Seller Central daily. If you face a serious suspension, consider professional help from a reputable consultant, and be careful about anyone who guarantees reinstatement.

Taxes for Amazon Sellers 13 min

This lesson is general education, not tax advice. Speak with a licensed accountant who understands online selling. Rules differ by state and situation.

Income tax

Your Amazon profit is taxable income, whether or not you receive a tax form. Profit equals your sales minus your costs of goods and business expenses. Sole proprietors and single member LLCs usually report this on a Schedule C with their personal return, and may owe self employment tax. Many sellers also make quarterly estimated payments.

Form 1099-K

Payment platforms report certain income to the IRS. For the 2026 tax year, the federal reporting threshold is reported as more than $20,000 in gross sales and more than 200 transactions. The form shows gross sales, not profit. You must report all business income even if you receive no form.

Sales tax

Amazon collects and remits sales tax for most orders in nearly every state with a sales tax, under marketplace facilitator laws. That does not remove every obligation. Depending on your state and where you hold inventory or reach sales thresholds, you may need to register for a sales tax permit or file returns. Holding FBA inventory in several states can create tax presence in those states, so ask your accountant.

Resale certificates

Wholesalers may ask for a resale certificate so you do not pay sales tax on inventory you resell.

Deductible expenses

Common business expenses include inventory cost, shipping, Amazon fees, advertising, software, supplies, and a portion of certain home office costs. Only deduct real, documented expenses.

Bookkeeping habits

  • Keep a separate business bank account and card.
  • Download Amazon reports monthly.
  • Save every invoice and receipt.
  • Track inventory on hand at year end.
  • Consider bookkeeping software or an accountant from the start. The cost is far less than the cost of a tax problem.

Module 13: Your 90 Day Launch Plan

Your 90 Day Plan From Zero to First Profit 18 min

This plan ties every module together. It is built to be followed week by week. Adjust it to your budget and pace.

Days 1 to 14: Foundation

  • Decide your structure, get an EIN, and open a business bank account.
  • Gather your documents and register your Professional account.
  • Complete verification and tour Seller Central.
  • Set your startup budget and your testing budget.

Days 15 to 30: Research

  • Contact suppliers, request invoices and samples, and vet them.
  • Choose one or two products to test.

Days 31 to 45: List and launch

  • Create strong listings with proper identifiers and good images.
  • Choose your fulfillment method for each product, either compliant dropshipping, merchant fulfillment, or FBA.
  • Place your first small inventory order or set up the supplier workflow, and test an order yourself.
  • Start a small automatic Sponsored Products campaign.

  • Fulfill orders on time and respond to messages in 24 hours.
  • Check account health daily.
  • Run the weekly ad loop.
  • Track your true profit after every fee on each product.

  • Improve the listing from the data: images, price, and keywords.
  • Move winning search terms to manual campaigns and add negative keywords.
  • Work on earning genuine reviews through great service and Amazon's compliant tools.
  • Review your Buy Box percentage and fix whatever is hurting it.

  • Compare each product to the targets you set at the start.
  • Reorder or scale winners. Cut or change losers.
  • Consider moving a winning product into wholesale or private label, and start the trademark.

  • Write down what you learned, and plan the next 90 days.

The honest truth

Most sellers do not reach large profits in 90 days, and that is normal. Seasonal Planning and Scaling Safely 13 min

Once you have a product that works, growth comes from doing the basics at a larger scale, without breaking what made it work.

Plan around the calendar

Expand your catalog carefully

Keep learning

Glossary of Amazon Seller Terms 8 min

Keep this glossary open while you work.

  • ACoS: advertising cost of sale. Ad spend divided by ad sales.
  • AWD: Amazon Warehousing and Distribution, a bulk storage service.
  • ASIN: Amazon Standard Identification Number, the ID for a product page.
  • A+ Content: enhanced product descriptions available through Brand Registry.
  • BSR: Best Sellers Rank, a ranking that reflects sales in a category.
  • Buy Box or Featured Offer: the purchase box where one offer is shown to the shopper.
  • FBA: Fulfillment by Amazon.
  • FBM: Fulfillment by Merchant, meaning you ship orders yourself.
  • FNSKU: Fulfillment Network Stock Keeping Unit, the barcode Amazon uses to track your units in FBA.
  • GTIN: Global Trade Item Number, such as a UPC, a barcode number for a product.
  • MCF: Multi Channel Fulfillment, using FBA inventory for orders from other places.
  • ODR: order defect rate, the share of orders with negative feedback, A to z claims, or chargebacks.
  • PPC: pay per click advertising.
  • Plan of action: your written response to an Amazon suspension.
  • Private label: products made for you and sold under your own brand.
  • ROAS: return on ad spend.
  • Seller Central: the dashboard where third party sellers manage their business.
  • Seller of record: the business shown as the seller of an order and responsible for it.
  • SFP: Seller Fulfilled Prime.
  • SKU: Stock Keeping Unit, your own code for a product.
  • Vendor Central: the system for first party suppliers who sell wholesale to Amazon by invitation.
  • Vine: Amazon's program that invites trusted reviewers to review new products.
